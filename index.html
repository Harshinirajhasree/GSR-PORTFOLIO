01

Introduction to GSR

Galvanic skin resistance is the electrical resistance of the skin. It falls when the sweat glands become more active, making it a simple, non-invasive index of emotional arousal.

🧠

Biofeedback

A body variable is measured, compared with a reference and fed back to the subject as a visual or audio signal, so the subject learns self-regulation. It is an educational process, not a treatment.

📈

Four Variables

Electrodermal activity, EMG, temperature and pulse are the variables commonly used in biofeedback. GSR belongs to electrodermal activity.

✋

Best Measured at

Palms and fingers, where the body has its highest concentration of sweat glands (R.S. Khandpur, Table 3.3).

02

Scientific Principle & Working Mechanism

A constant dc current is passed between an active and a neutral silver electrode. The voltage across the skin is proportional to its resistance, so R = V / I (Ohm's law).

Ohm's Law Explorer

I (held constant, illustrative) = 10 µA  →  V = 1.00 V

Because I is constant, the measured V directly tracks R.

BSR and GSR: Slow Baseline + Fast Change

  • BSR basal skin resistance: the average activity of the sweat glands
  • GSR galvanic skin response: the quick high and low points caused by a stimulus

06

RC Circuit & Signal Processing

The BSR output passes through an RC network with a time constant of 3 to 5 seconds. The slow baseline is blocked and only the rapid change, the GSR, reaches amplifier A2 and its meter.

  • Input from A1: slow drift + quick responses
  • After C–R: baseline blocked, only quick change passes

08

Relaxation vs Stress: Visual Comparison

When relaxed, sweat gland activity falls and resistance rises. When tense or excited, sweating increases and resistance falls.

😌 Relaxed

  • Sweat gland activity: low
  • Skin resistance: high
  • LEDs: green bar
  • Audio tone: low pitch

😣 Tense

  • Sweat gland activity: high
  • Skin resistance: low
  • LEDs: red lit
  • Audio tone: high pitch
